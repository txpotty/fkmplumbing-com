    Water lines can leak or break due to several factors, including aging pipes (corrosion in galvanized steel or copper), shifting soil, tree root intrusion, high water pressure, ground freezing and thawing cycles, and accidental damage during excavation. Poor initial installation can also lead to early failure.

    The duration of a water line repair depends on the severity and type of repair. A simple spot repair might take a few hours, while a more extensive replacement, especially if it involves excavation or trenchless methods, could take one to three days. We always strive to complete the work efficiently while maintaining solid quality.

    Trenchless water line repair is a method of repairing or replacing underground pipes without extensive digging. It involves creating small access points and using specialized equipment to either pull a new pipe through the old one (pipe bursting) or insert a new lining into the existing pipe (pipe lining). This method saves landscaping and reduces repair time.

    Common signs of a water line leak include an unexplained increase in your water bill, damp or soggy spots in your yard (even during dry weather), a noticeable drop in water pressure, discolored water, or the sound of running water when no fixtures are in use. If you notice any of these, it's wise to call a professional plumber promptly.

    A homeowner can sometimes tighten a leaking fitting at an exposed hose bib. Buried service line repairs require locating, safe excavation, 811 utility marking and code-approved fittings. Mistakes can cause bigger leaks, so most homeowners hire a licensed plumber.
