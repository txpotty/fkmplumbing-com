    Anode Rod Inspection & Replacement in Laguna Niguel, CA

    The anode rod is your hot water tank's sacrificial protector against corrosion. Municipal water across much of Southern California is hard, leaving chalky scale on faucets, showerheads, and inside water heater tanks. During our maintenance, we pull and inspect the anode rod. If it's significantly depleted, we recommend and perform a replacement to extend the life of your tank in Laguna Niguel, CA, preventing rust from eating away at the tank lining.

    Gas Burner and Venting Tune-Up in Laguna Niguel, CA

    Frequently Asked Questions About Hot Water Tank Maintenance in Laguna Niguel

    A standard flush and inspection typically runs $125 to $250 depending on tank size and access. Anode replacement usually adds $75 to $200 in parts and labor, and powered anodes cost more.

    Many manufacturers, including Rheem, A.O. Smith and Bradford White, expect regular maintenance and can deny claims for sediment damage. Keeping service records protects your warranty.

    The anode rod, also known as a sacrificial rod, is a metal rod inside your hot water tank designed to corrode before the tank itself. It protects the tank lining from rust and corrosion, which counts most when water carries a lot of minerals, and it extends the lifespan of your unit.

    Neglecting maintenance can lead to poor operation, higher energy bills, premature tank failure from corrosion or sediment buildup, and potential safety hazards from a malfunctioning pressure relief valve. Regular service heads off these costly and dangerous issues.

    Most manufacturers and plumbing experts recommend annual hot water tank maintenance. This is particularly true where water is hard, since minerals speed up sediment buildup and anode rod depletion.

    Once a year is the baseline for most homes, and more often where water is hard. Homes without a softener or with heavy use may benefit from a flush every six months.

    Common signs include reduced hot water supply, odd noises like rumbling or popping, rust-colored water, a sulfur or rotten egg smell, or water leaking from the tank. These point to issues that maintenance can often resolve.

    In mineral-heavy water a magnesium rod may last three to five years. We measure it at every visit so you replace it before the tank lining starts to rust.
