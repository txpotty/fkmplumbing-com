    Frequently Asked Questions About Underground Pipe Repair in Carson

    The cost of underground pipe repair in Carson, CA can vary a lot, typically ranging from $500 for minor spot repairs to $3,000 to $10,000 or more for trenchless full line replacements, or even more for extensive excavation and re-piping. Factors include the pipe material, depth, length of the damaged section, the repair method chosen and the extent of property restoration needed. We provide transparent, upfront pricing after a thorough diagnosis.

    The duration of an underground pipe repair depends heavily on the severity and location of the damage, as well as the repair method chosen. Minor repairs using trenchless techniques might be completed in a single day. More extensive issues, such as full sewer line replacements requiring excavation, could take two to five days, including restoration work.

    FKM Plumbing is equipped to repair a wide range of underground pipe materials, including PVC, ABS, cast iron, copper, PEX and even older materials like clay or polybutylene. Our technicians are trained on the right repair techniques for each material.

    Whether an underground pipe leak is covered by insurance depends on your homeowner's policy and the cause of the damage. Typically, sudden and accidental damage may be covered, but issues from normal wear and tear or neglect are generally not. It's best to review your policy or contact your insurance provider directly for clarification.

    Common signs include a water bill that jumps without explanation, a meter that moves with all water off, low pressure and wet or sinking spots in the yard. A hissing sound near the meter box can also point to a leak.

    Trenchless pipe repair offers several key advantages, including minimal disruption to your yard, landscaping and concrete, which can save on restoration costs. It's usually faster than traditional excavation and less invasive, preserving the look and value of your property. This method is also durable, often extending the lifespan of your pipes.

    In most cities, the homeowner owns and maintains the water line from the meter to the house. The utility is responsible for the meter and the line on the street side. We can help confirm which side the leak is on.
