    Well Pressure Tank Leak Repair vs. Replacement in Torrance

    When your well pressure tank shows signs of trouble, it is important to determine if a repair is practical or if full replacement is necessary. Small air leaks or issues with the air valve might be repairable. However, if the tank itself is corroded, has a ruptured diaphragm, or is waterlogged, replacement is almost always the more cost-effective and reliable option. Our technicians in Torrance provide an honest assessment, explaining the pros and cons of repair versus a complete well pressure tank replacement, so you can make an informed decision.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Torrance, CA

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop, typically at 30/50 or 40/60 psi. Pitted contacts, insect nests and a clogged sensing nipple are common causes of failure in well houses. We replace worn switches with Square D or similar models and clean or replace the nipple below it. A low-pressure cutoff switch can also be added to stop the pump if the well runs dry. We set the switch to suit your new tank's pre-charge.

    Bladder Pressure Tank Replacement in Torrance

    Bladder tanks hold water inside a flexible butyl bag surrounded by pressurized air. When the bag ruptures, water fills the whole tank, there is no air cushion and the pump short cycles. A simple test is pressing the air valve on top, since water spitting out means the bladder has failed. We replace the tank, set the new pre-charge and verify the pump runs a full cycle. Most bladder tank swaps take about two to three hours.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Torrance

    The cost for well pressure tank replacement in Torrance, CA usually ranges from $700 to $1,800, depending on the tank size, brand, and complexity of the installation. This includes the tank itself, labor, and any necessary fittings. We provide a detailed quote after assessing your specific system needs.

    Regular maintenance for a well pressure tank involves checking the air pressure in the tank at least once a year, ensuring it's properly pre-charged. You should also listen for excessive pump cycling and visually inspect the tank for any signs of corrosion or leaks. We can include this check during routine well system servicing.

    Most pressure tanks last 10 to 15 years. Hard water, sand and frequent cycling can shorten that, especially when the pre-charge is never checked.

    Rapid pump cycling, pressure that swings up and down, and water coming out of the air valve are typical signs. A tank that sounds solid and full when tapped near the top is usually waterlogged.

    Most pressure tank replacements take two to four hours. Adding a constant pressure system or multiple tanks can take the better part of a day.

    We install a variety of trusted well pressure tank brands, including Well-Rite, Flex-Lite, Amtrol, and Goulds. Our technicians can recommend the best brand and model for your specific well system and budget.

    FKM Plumbing offers a comprehensive warranty on both the well pressure tank itself (as per manufacturer's warranty) and our installation labor. We stand behind our work to ensure your full satisfaction and peace of mind.

    Both bladder and diaphragm tanks use an air cushion to pressurize water, but they differ in how the water and air are separated. Bladder tanks have a replaceable rubber bladder that holds the water, fully isolating it from the air. Diaphragm tanks use a fixed rubber diaphragm. Bladder tanks are generally preferred for their longevity and ease of maintenance.
