    Most disposals last 8 to 12 years. Hard water, heavy use and grease can cut that life.

    For light use in a single-person household, 1/3 HP might be enough. Most homeowners opt for 1/2 HP or 3/4 HP for general use, which handles common food waste well. For large families, frequent cooking, or tougher waste like small bones, a 1 HP unit offers the best performance and durability.

    Most replacements take about an hour. Upgrades with new electrical or drain changes may take longer.

    Most garbage disposal replacements can be completed within 1 to 2 hours. This timeframe includes disconnecting the old unit, installing the new one, and testing it carefully.     Common signs include frequent clogs, loud grinding noises without processing food, persistent leaks from the unit's housing, or if the disposal simply stops turning on. If you notice these issues, it's likely time for a replacement.

    To extend the life of your new disposal, avoid putting fibrous foods like celery, corn husks, or onion skins down it. Also, do not dispose of grease, coffee grounds, or non-food items.
