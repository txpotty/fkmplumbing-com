    Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in Upland

    The cost of a whole-home repipe in Upland, CA can vary significantly depending on the size of your home, the number of fixtures, and the chosen piping material, such as copper or PEX. Generally, you can expect to pay anywhere from $4,000 to $15,000 or more. We provide a detailed, upfront quote after a thorough assessment of your specific home and needs.

    Repeated slab leaks, pinhole leaks, low pressure, discolored water or polybutylene, Kitec or galvanized pipe are all signs. Frequent leak repairs are the strongest signal.

    Municipal water across much of Southern California is hard, leaving chalky scale on faucets, showerheads, and inside water heater tanks. Hard water can lead to mineral buildup, or scale, inside pipes over time. This buildup constricts water flow, reduces pressure, and can speed up corrosion, especially in older metal pipes. Repiping with materials like PEX or properly installed copper, along with considering a water softener, can reduce these effects and extend the life of your plumbing system.

    Copper is a rigid, durable metal with a long lifespan, known for its resistance to bacteria. PEX is a flexible plastic tubing that is more affordable, easier to install, resistant to freezing and corrosion, and very quiet. Both are strong choices for repiping, and our specialists can help you decide which material is best suited for your home and budget in Upland, CA.

    Common signs you need repiping include persistent low water pressure throughout your home, discolored or rusty-looking water, frequent pipe leaks, visible corrosion on exposed pipes, and a metallic taste or odor in your water. If your home is over 50 years old with original pipes, it's a good idea to have them inspected, especially if they are galvanized or polybutylene.

    We commonly use Uponor PEX-A with ProPEX fittings and Type L copper from domestic mills. We also use Viega ProPress fittings for copper repipes.

    PEX resists scale and pinhole corrosion and handles expansion well, which makes it a strong choice for hard water. Copper is durable too but costs more and can pinhole in aggressive water.
