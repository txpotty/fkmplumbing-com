    Key signs include the pump not turning on, unusual noises such as grinding or rattling, running continuously, visible rust or corrosion, or an age over 7 to 10 years. Any of these could point to coming failure and a need for replacement.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, powered by your home's electricity. A backup sump pump, usually battery-powered or water-powered, is a secondary system that activates if the primary pump fails, loses power or cannot keep up with heavy water inflow. Many Hemet homes are better off with a backup system due to storm risks.

    Constant running, loud grinding, frequent cycling or failure to start are typical signs. Rust, age and a burning smell also indicate trouble.

    It is a good idea to test your sump pump at least every three months, and especially before and after rainy seasons. To test, simply pour a bucket of water into the sump pit.     Sump pumps usually have a lifespan of 7 to 10 years. Even if they appear to be working, their efficiency can decrease over time. Regular inspection and testing are important, and replacement is advisable as they approach the end of their expected service life.

    A battery backup sump pump system is a secondary pump powered by a battery, designed to turn on automatically if your primary sump pump loses power during an outage or fails. This is critical for maintaining flood protection during power outages caused by severe weather.
