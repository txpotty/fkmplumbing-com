    In most communities the water provider owns the meter and the pipe on the street side of it. The homeowner is responsible for the service line from the meter to the house. We confirm which side the leak is on before quoting you.

    Full service line replacement usually requires a plumbing permit and inspection from your city or county. Small spot repairs often do not. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection when needed.

    Water lines can leak or break due to several factors, including aging pipes (corrosion in galvanized steel or copper), shifting soil, tree root intrusion, high water pressure, ground freezing and thawing cycles, and accidental damage during excavation. Poor initial installation can also lead to early failure.

    Common signs of a broken water line include surprisingly high water bills, a sudden drop in water pressure, wet spots in your yard or on pavement that don't dry, discolored water, or the sound of running water when no fixtures are in use. For homes on slab foundations, watch for cracks in the foundation or warm spots on the floor.

    Preventing future water line issues involves a few key steps: being mindful of landscaping near buried lines, quickly fixing minor leaks, and considering a whole-home water pressure regulator if your pressure is consistently high. Regular plumbing inspections can also help identify potential problems before they escalate. For older homes in Hemet, proactive pipe replacement might be the best long-term solution.
