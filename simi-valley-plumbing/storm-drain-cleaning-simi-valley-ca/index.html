    Frequently Asked Questions About Storm Drain Cleaning in Simi Valley

    Residential storm drain cleaning in Simi Valley usually runs $200 to $500 depending on the number of inlets and pipe length. Dry well cleaning and commercial systems typically cost more and are quoted after a site visit.

    While you can remove surface debris from drain grates, deeply clogged storm drains need professional tools like hydro jetters and camera equipment for effective cleaning. DIY attempts can sometimes push debris further down or damage the pipes, leading to more costly repairs. It is safer and more effective to hire a professional.

    To prevent storm drain clogs, regularly clear leaves and debris from grates and catch basins, avoid planting trees directly over drain lines, and keep landscaping in good shape to prevent soil erosion. Consider installing grates with smaller openings in high-debris areas and schedule annual professional cleanings.

    If our video inspection shows that your storm drain is damaged, such as collapsed, cracked, or severely invaded by roots, cleaning alone will not solve the problem. We will provide a detailed assessment and discuss repair or replacement options, including trenchless methods where appropriate, for a long-term solution for your Simi Valley property.

    In Simi Valley, common causes of storm drain clogs include built-up leaves, dirt, and organic debris from trees, silt and sediment from soil erosion, trash, and sadly, sometimes even illegally dumped waste. Tree root intrusion into the pipes is also a significant factor, especially in older systems.

    You can clear grates and remove surface debris with a scoop. Pipes packed with silt and gravel usually need jetting and vacuum equipment.

    It is generally recommended to have your storm drains inspected and cleaned at least once a year. Properties with many trees or those that see frequent heavy rains may need twice-yearly cleaning to prevent buildup and blockages.

    Most homes take 1 to 3 hours. Large commercial sites or dry wells may take a half day or more.
