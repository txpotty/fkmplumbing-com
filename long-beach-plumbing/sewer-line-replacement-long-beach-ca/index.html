    Trenchless Sewer Line Replacement in Long Beach, CA

    Open-Trench Sewer Replacement in Long Beach, CA

    Traditional excavation is sometimes the best option, especially when the old line has a severe belly or the slope must be corrected. Each joint is glued and checked before backfill. The inspector sees the open trench before we cover it.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewer Line Replacement in Long Beach

    Sewer line replacement in Long Beach typically ranges from about $4,000 to $15,000 for a typical residential lateral, and can run higher for long, deep or hard-to-reach lines. Trenchless work is often priced around $80 to $250 per foot depending on conditions.

    The timeline for sewer line replacement depends on the method and scope of work. A trenchless replacement can often be completed in 1 to 3 days, while a traditional excavation might take 3 to 5 days, sometimes longer for large projects or difficult access. We will provide an estimated timeline during your consultation.

    With trenchless methods, typically only two small pits are dug. Open-trench work disturbs more, but we backfill and restore the area when we are done.

    Modern sewer lines made from materials like PVC or HDPE, when properly installed, can last 50 to 100 years or longer. This is a big improvement over older materials like cast iron or clay, which typically have a shorter lifespan.

    Traditional replacement involves digging a trench to access and replace the entire pipe, which can be more invasive to your yard. Trenchless methods, like pipe bursting or pipe lining, require minimal digging, often just two access points, protecting your landscaping while installing a new pipe.

    Sewer line replacement is a demanding job that requires specialized equipment, permits, and expertise in excavation, pipe fitting, and local plumbing codes. It is not a good DIY project and should always be handled by licensed, experienced professionals for safety and compliance.

    We primarily use high-quality PVC (polyvinyl chloride) or HDPE (high-density polyethylene) pipe for sewer line replacements. These materials are durable, corrosion-resistant, flexible, and long-lived, making them well suited for the soil conditions in Southern California.

    Most policies do not cover wear and tear or root damage to sewer lines. Some offer service line coverage as an add-on, so review your policy.
