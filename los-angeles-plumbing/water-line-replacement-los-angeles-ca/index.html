    Main Water Line Repair vs. Replacement in Los Angeles

    Deciding between repair and full replacement depends on the extent of the damage. If your main water line in Los Angeles has multiple leaks, severe corrosion, or is nearing the end of its lifespan, replacement is often the more sensible long-term solution. Our technicians will perform a complete inspection, including pressure testing and possibly video inspection, to recommend the best course of action.     Traditional Water Line Excavation in Los Angeles

    Sometimes, traditional open-trench excavation is the most appropriate method for water line replacement in Los Angeles, especially for severe damage, complex pipe layouts, or when the existing pipe material is incompatible with trenchless techniques. Our team carefully excavates the area, removes the old pipe, and installs a new, high-quality main water line, typically using copper or PEX.     Trenchless Water Line Replacement in Los Angeles, CA

    For homeowners looking to minimize disruption to their landscaping, trenchless water line replacement offers an excellent alternative. Using pipe bursting or pipe pulling techniques, we can install a new water line with minimal excavation, typically requiring only two small access points. This method is well suited for properties in Los Angeles with mature trees, detailed landscaping, or concrete driveways, saving time and restoration costs.

    PEX Water Line Replacement in Los Angeles

    PEX (cross-linked polyethylene) is a popular and budget-friendly choice for water line replacement in Los Angeles. It's flexible, resistant to freezing, and less prone to scaling and corrosion than some traditional materials. Its flexibility allows for easier installation, often requiring fewer connections, which reduces the potential for leaks. PEX is also a good option for homes with corrosive soil conditions, offering a reliable alternative to metal pipes.

    Low pressure, rusty water, wet spots in the yard and high water bills are typical signs. Frequent leaks also show the line is failing.
