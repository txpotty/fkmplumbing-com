    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Replacement in Los Angeles

    The cost of water heater replacement in Los Angeles, CA typically ranges from $1,200 to $2,500 for a standard tank unit, including installation. Tankless units can range from $3,000 to $5,000 or more, depending on the model and needed plumbing modifications. Factors like unit type, capacity, brand and labor complexity affect the final price.

    We install Bradford White, Rheem and A.O. Smith water heaters. Each offers dependable performance and a range of warranties.

    Manufacturer tank warranties typically range from 6 to 12 years depending on the model. We also warranty our installation labor.

    Generally, if your water heater is over 10 years old, frequently breaks down or has a major issue like a leaking tank, replacement is often more cost-effective in the long run. For newer units with minor, easily fixable issues, repair might be a viable option. Our technicians can provide an honest assessment.

    FKM Plumbing installs and services all major brands, including Bradford White, Rheem, AO Smith, Rinnai, Navien and State Water Heaters. We can help you choose the best brand and model that suits your budget and hot water demands.

    While it might seem tempting, water heater replacement involves gas lines, high voltage electricity, water pressure and heavy equipment. It is a complex job best handled by licensed professionals to ensure safety, proper installation and compliance with local plumbing codes, preventing potential leaks or hazards.

    Common signs include a lack of hot water, rust-colored water, a metallic taste in hot water, strange noises like rumbling or popping from the tank, visible leaks around the base of the unit or a water heater that is over 10 to 12 years old. If you notice any of these, it is time to call a licensed plumber.

    A traditional water heater stores and heats a limited amount of water in a tank, whereas a tankless water heater heats water on demand as it flows through the unit. Tankless units offer endless hot water and can be more energy-efficient, but usually have a higher upfront cost and may require gas line upgrades.
