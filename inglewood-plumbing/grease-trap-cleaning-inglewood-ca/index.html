    The duration of a grease trap cleaning can vary, but most standard commercial cleanings take between 1 to 3 hours. Larger outdoor interceptors or traps with significant buildup may take longer.     Key signs include slow drains in your kitchen sink, gurgling noises from drains, foul odors coming from your drains or around the trap area, and visible FOG buildup when you inspect the trap. Ignoring these can lead to complete blockages.

    Yes, we provide a service record after every cleaning. It includes the date, volume removed and condition of the trap, which you can show the inspector.

    FOG stands for Fats, Oils, and Grease. When poured down drains, FOG cools and solidifies, sticking to pipe walls and eventually leading to severe clogs. In grease traps, it separates from wastewater, but if not regularly removed, it overflows into the sewer system, causing blockages and environmental issues.

    The terms are often used interchangeably, but 'grease interceptor' usually refers to larger, outdoor units with capacities over 500 gallons, while 'grease trap' usually describes smaller, indoor units under 100 gallons, such as under-sink models. Both serve the same function of separating FOG from wastewater.

    Slow sink drains, foul odors, grease on the water surface near the outlet and backups from floor drains are common signs. If you notice any of these, schedule a cleaning right away.
