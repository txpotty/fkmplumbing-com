    Slab leaks can cause serious damage including foundation cracks, soil erosion beneath the slab, mold and mildew growth in wall cavities or under flooring, damage to flooring materials and increased humidity levels. Over time, it can lead to structural instability and extensive, costly repairs.

    Expansive clay and hillside soils can shift over time, putting stress on slab plumbing and cracking older sewer joints. Hard water can also corrode copper pipes from the inside. Pipes can rub against concrete or gravel as they expand with hot water, and over time these factors cause pinholes and cracks.

    While the detection process itself usually doesn't require a permit, any subsequent repair work involving the excavation of the slab or significant plumbing alterations will likely require a plumbing permit from the local building department.     Common signs of a slab leak include surprisingly high water bills, warm spots on your floor, the sound of running water when no fixtures are in use, visible cracks in the foundation, damp or wet spots on carpets or flooring and a sudden drop in water pressure. Musty odors can also point to moisture buildup.

    Common pipe materials prone to slab leaks include older copper pipes vulnerable to corrosion from hard water or soil acidity, and sometimes PEX or CPVC if they were improperly installed or exposed to extreme temperatures.
