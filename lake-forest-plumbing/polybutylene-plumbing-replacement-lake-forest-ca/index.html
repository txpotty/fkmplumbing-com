    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Lake Forest

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper costs more, and yard service lines add $1,500 to $4,000.

    Removing and replacing an entire supply system requires routing, pressure testing and permits. A licensed crew finishes faster and provides warranties and paperwork.

    Both PEX and copper are excellent choices. PEX is flexible, less expensive, more freeze-resistant, and handles ground movement better than rigid pipe. Copper offers superior rigidity, a long lifespan, and a strong track record. The 'better' choice depends on your budget, preferences, and home's particular needs.

    We use quality, industry-leading brands for our replacement materials, such as Uponor AquaPEX or Viega PEX for PEX systems, and reputable manufacturers for copper piping. We make sure all materials meet or exceed industry standards for durability and performance.

    Municipal water across much of Southern California is hard, leaving white scale on faucets, showerheads, and inside water heater tanks. While hard water doesn't directly cause polybutylene to fail like chlorine does, it can contribute to scale buildup within the pipes. This buildup can reduce water pressure and potentially worsen problems if the pipe material is already weakened.

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water break down the plastic from the inside, making it brittle. Fittings and hot water lines often fail before the rest.
