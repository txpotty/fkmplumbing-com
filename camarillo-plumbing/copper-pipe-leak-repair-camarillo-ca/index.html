    Insurance frequently covers sudden water damage but not the cost of repairing the pipe itself. Review your policy, and we will provide documentation to help with a claim.

    Type K, L and M copper pipes differ primarily in their wall thickness and thus their pressure ratings. Type K has the thickest walls and is used for underground service and main water lines. Type L is common for interior water supply and radiant heating. Type M has the thinnest walls and is usually used for less demanding applications. We are skilled in repairing all types.

    We typically use Type L copper, which has thicker walls than Type M and lasts longer. In some situations we use ProPress fittings or a PEX transition.

    Copper pipes can leak due to several factors, including corrosion from water chemistry when the pH is too acidic or alkaline, aggressive soil conditions, high water pressure, poor installation or physical damage from foundation shifts. Around Camarillo, flushing the water heater yearly and cleaning aerators keeps scale buildup from cutting flow and efficiency. Pinhole leaks usually result from long-term internal corrosion.

    Common signs of a copper pipe leak include unexplained wet spots on walls, ceilings or floors, a sudden drop in water pressure, the sound of running water when no fixtures are in use, unexpectedly high water bills or visible blue-green stains on pipes or nearby surfaces that point to corrosion.

    Most standard copper pipe leak repairs, such as fixing a visible pinhole leak or replacing a small section, can be completed within two to four hours.
