    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in San Clemente

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper costs more, and yard service lines typically add $1,500 to $4,000.

    Common signs include frequent leaks, discolored water, low water pressure, or visible grey or blue plastic pipes often marked 'PB2110'. If your home was built between 1978 and 1995 and has these pipes, replacement is strongly advised.

    Properly installed PEX piping typically has a lifespan of 25 to 50 years, while copper piping can last 50 to 70 years or even longer. Both are significant upgrades from polybutylene and offer decades of reliable service for your San Clemente, CA home.

    Municipal water across much of Southern California is hard, leaving chalky scale on faucets, showerheads, and inside water heater tanks. While hard water doesn't directly cause polybutylene to fail like chlorine does, it can contribute to scale buildup within the pipes. This buildup can reduce water pressure and potentially add to problems if the pipe material is already compromised.

    Most policies do not pay for replacing old pipe, though they may cover sudden water damage. Some insurers raise rates or decline coverage for homes with polybutylene.

    We use quality, industry-leading brands for our replacement materials, such as Uponor AquaPEX or Viega PEX for PEX systems, and reputable manufacturers for copper piping. We ensure all materials meet or exceed industry standards for durability and performance.

    Both PEX and copper are excellent choices. PEX is flexible, less expensive, more freeze-resistant, and handles ground movement better than rigid pipe. Copper offers superior rigidity, a long lifespan, and a strong track record. The 'better' choice depends on your budget, preferences, and home's particular needs.

    Polybutylene pipe replacement is a complex plumbing project that requires specialized tools, knowledge of plumbing codes, and real experience. Attempting a DIY replacement can lead to costly mistakes, leaks, and potential code violations. It is best left to licensed professionals.
