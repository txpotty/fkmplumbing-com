    Double Check Valve Assembly (DCVA) Installation in San Clemente

    The double check valve assembly, or DCVA, is a common backflow preventer for low-hazard applications where the consequences of backflow are not severe. In San Clemente, DCVAs are often installed on fire sprinkler systems or irrigation lines. Our certified plumbers carefully install these devices, making sure both check valves operate correctly and are properly sealed to prevent backpressure or back-siphonage into the main water supply.

    Reduced Pressure Zone Assembly Installation in San Clemente, CA

    RPZ assemblies offer the highest level of protection and are required for high hazard connections like commercial kitchens, medical facilities, chemical injection and car washes. They include two independent check valves with a relief valve that dumps water if either check fails. Because the relief valve can discharge, we plan for drainage and install the assembly above grade where it can be tested easily. We carry trusted brands like Watts, Febco, Wilkins and Apollo.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer Installation in San Clemente

    Residential pressure vacuum breakers typically cost $250 to $700 installed. Double check and RPZ assemblies for larger homes or businesses range from $800 to $3,500 or more depending on size and enclosure needs.

    An RPZ, or reduced pressure zone device, provides the highest level of protection and is used for high-hazard applications where contaminants could be toxic. A DCVA, or double check valve assembly, is used for low-hazard applications where contaminants are non-toxic. The choice depends on the risk of contamination at the cross-connection point.

    In San Clemente, most backflow preventers must be tested annually by a certified backflow tester to confirm they are working correctly and providing adequate protection. This is a separate service from installation but is crucial for ongoing compliance and safety.

    You probably need a backflow preventer if you have an irrigation system, a fire sprinkler system, a swimming pool or any auxiliary water source that connects to the main water supply. Local regulations in San Clemente often require these devices for certain applications to protect public health.

    Most water providers require testable assemblies to be tested at installation and then every year. Some high hazard facilities may be tested more often.
