    New Sump Pit Excavation in Corona

    If your basement has never had a pump, we start by locating the low point where water collects. We saw cut the slab, excavate a pit roughly 18 to 24 inches wide and 24 to 30 inches deep, and set a perforated or solid liner depending on whether we are collecting groundwater or surface seepage. Gravel goes around the liner to help water flow in while keeping fine soil out. We then patch the slab flush with new concrete so the floor stays clean and level.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Corona

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit typically runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab generally falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    Swapping a pump in an existing pit usually takes two to three hours. A new pit with slab cutting and concrete patching usually takes most of a day.

    In many areas, including Corona, a plumbing permit may be required for new sump pump installations or major replacements to confirm compliance with local building codes. FKM Plumbing will help you work through the permit process and make sure all work is up to code.

    Watch for a pump that runs constantly, cycles on and off rapidly, makes grinding noises or never seems to turn on. Rust stains, a stuck float and water sitting above the switch level are also warning signs.

    Discharge should go to your yard, a dry well or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Connecting a sump pump to the sanitary sewer is generally not allowed by local codes.

    Copper pinhole leaks and corroded cast iron drains are frequent repair calls in homes that are now several decades old. Homes built on slabs without basements rarely need one. Homes that do have basements or below-grade rooms can take on water fast during heavy storms, especially where the soil sheds rain instead of absorbing it.

    When heavy winter storms do arrive, runoff can overwhelm yard drains and storm systems, and burn areas can send mud into low-lying homes. Because storms and power outages tend to arrive together, a battery backup sump pump is strongly recommended. It offers crucial protection by switching on automatically when the main power fails, so your basement stays dry even without electricity. It is a wise investment for peace of mind.

    While it might seem like a DIY project, basement sump pump installation involves complex plumbing, electrical work and proper discharge routing. Incorrect installation can lead to pump failure, electrical hazards or poor water removal, which can cause serious flood damage. It is always best to hire a licensed plumber for a proper, code-compliant installation in Corona.
