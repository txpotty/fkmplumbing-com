    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Replacement in Palmdale

    The cost of water heater replacement in Palmdale, CA typically ranges from $1,200 to $2,500 for a standard tank unit, including installation. Tankless units can range from $3,000 to $5,000 or more, depending on the model and necessary plumbing modifications. Factors like unit type, capacity, brand and labor complexity affect the final price.

    Most new water heaters come with a manufacturer's warranty ranging from 6 to 12 years on the tank and components, depending on the model and brand. FKM Plumbing also provides a labor warranty on our installation, so your investment is protected.

    Yes, in many areas of Palmdale and Southern California, a plumbing permit is required for water heater replacement to make sure the installation meets local safety and building codes. FKM Plumbing handles all necessary permitting on your behalf, so you do not have to worry about compliance.

    FKM Plumbing installs and services all major brands, including Bradford White, Rheem, AO Smith, Rinnai, Navien and State Water Heaters. We can help you choose the best brand and model that fits your budget and hot water demands.

    Flushing a traditional tank water heater annually is recommended in most homes. Around Palmdale, flushing the water heater yearly and cleaning aerators keeps mineral buildup from cutting flow and efficiency. This helps remove sediment buildup, which can reduce efficiency and shorten the lifespan of the unit. Regular flushing can also prevent scale from damaging heating elements.

    Common signs include a lack of hot water, rust-colored water, a metallic taste in hot water, strange noises like rumbling or popping from the tank, visible leaks around the base of the unit or a water heater that is over 10 to 12 years old. If you notice any of these, it is time to call a professional.

    A traditional water heater stores and heats a limited amount of water in a tank, whereas a tankless water heater heats water on demand as it flows through the unit. Tankless units offer endless hot water and can be more energy-efficient, but usually have a higher upfront cost and may require gas line upgrades.
