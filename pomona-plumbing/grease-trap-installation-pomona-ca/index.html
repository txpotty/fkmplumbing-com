    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Pomona

    The cost of grease trap installation in Pomona, CA varies widely based on the type and size of the trap, whether it's an indoor or outdoor unit, and the complexity of the installation. For a small under-sink hydro-mechanical unit, expect to pay between $1,500 and $4,000. Larger outdoor gravity interceptors can range from $7,000 to $20,000 or more, including excavation and concrete work.

    Signs you might need a new grease trap include frequent drain clogs, slow drainage in multiple fixtures, persistent foul odors coming from drains, or if your current unit is old, corroded, or no longer meeting local code requirements. An inspection by a professional plumber can confirm the need for replacement.

    The timeline for grease trap installation depends on the project's scope. A simple under-sink unit might take a few hours. Larger outdoor gravity interceptors, which involve excavation and more extensive plumbing, can take anywhere from one to several days. We always aim to minimize disruption to your business operations.

    We recommend and install high-quality, durable grease trap brands such as Zurn, Big Dipper, Schier, and Rockford Separators. These manufacturers are known for their reliability and compliance with industry standards, offering various solutions to meet diverse commercial kitchen needs.

    The lifespan of a grease trap depends on its type, material, and how well it's maintained. Steel grease traps may last 10 to 15 years, while durable fiberglass or polyethylene units can last 20 to 30 years or even longer. Regular cleaning and proper usage are key to lengthening its lifespan.

    While the terms are often used interchangeably, 'grease trap' usually refers to smaller, indoor hydro-mechanical units, often found under sinks. 'Grease interceptor' usually refers to larger, outdoor gravity-fed units with higher capacities. Both serve the same purpose of separating FOG from wastewater.

    Most food service businesses that cook, wash dishes or prepare food with fats, oils or grease are required to have one. Local pretreatment programs set the specific rules, and we can help you find out what applies to your site.
