    Common signs include visible cracks or chips in the ceramic, constant leaking at the base that a new wax ring won't fix, a wobbly toilet, or a very old toilet that often clogs or runs continuously.

    If your toilet is older than 20 years, uses a lot of water or the bowl is hard to source, replacing the whole toilet is usually smarter. If the tank is newer and works well, a bowl swap can save money.

    Small cosmetic chips above the waterline can be patched, but cracks below the waterline or near bolt holes cannot be reliably repaired. Replacement is the safe choice.

    Hard water shortens the life of tankless units, valves, and appliances, which is why many homes in Southern California add softeners or scale filters. Hard water can lead to mineral buildup and tough stains inside your toilet bowl, particularly around the waterline and jets. Over time, these mineral deposits can also affect flushing efficiency.     Key signs you need a new toilet bowl include visible cracks, persistent leaks that aren't resolved by simple repairs, severe and unremovable stains, a wobbly toilet due to a damaged bowl or flange, or a bowl that is outdated and inefficient.
