    Metered Softener Replacement in Pomona

    Older timer-based softeners regenerate on a set schedule whether you used water or not. Metered softeners track actual usage and regenerate only when the resin is nearly exhausted. This saves salt, water and wear on the system. We replace old timer valves with metered Fleck 5600SXT or Clack WS1 valves. Most homeowners notice lower salt use within the first month.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Softener Replacement in Pomona

    The cost of water softener replacement in Pomona, CA can vary widely, typically ranging from $1,500 to $4,000. This depends on the type and size of the new system, labor costs and any needed plumbing changes. We provide clear, upfront pricing after assessing your needs.

    While it may seem like a straightforward task, water softener replacement involves working with water lines, drain connections and sometimes electrical components. Improper installation can lead to leaks, inefficient operation or even water damage. Hiring a professional ensures the job is done correctly, safely and to code.

    Salt-free conditioners reduce scale buildup but do not remove hardness minerals. A true softener removes them, which is more effective for soap and fixtures.

    Most softener replacements take two to four hours. New loops or drain lines can take longer.

    With proper maintenance, most quality water softeners can last between 10 and 15 years, and sometimes even longer. Factors like water hardness, usage volume and the quality of the original installation can affect its lifespan. Regular servicing helps lengthen the life of your unit.

    A new, properly sized water softener can sometimes improve water pressure if your old unit was undersized or had heavy mineral buildup restricting flow. However, a water softener's main job is to remove hardness minerals, not to boost pressure. If low water pressure is your main concern, we can investigate other causes.

    Most softeners last 10 to 15 years. Resin and valves wear out sooner with very hard or chlorinated water.

    We test your hardness in grains per gallon and estimate daily water usage. That determines the resin capacity needed.
