    Cured-in-Place Pipe Lining in Pomona

    CIPP lining involves cleaning the old pipe, then inserting a flexible liner saturated with epoxy resin. The liner is inflated against the pipe wall and cured with hot water, steam or UV light. Once hardened, it forms a smooth, jointless pipe inside the old one. Lining works best for cracks, root intrusion and separated joints.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Trenchless Pipe Repair in Pomona

    The cost of trenchless pipe repair in Pomona, CA can vary widely, usually ranging from $80 to $200 per linear foot, depending on the pipe's diameter, length of the repair, and the specific trenchless method used. While often higher initially than traditional digging, it usually saves money in the long run by avoiding extensive landscaping and property restoration costs. A full estimate will be provided after an inspection.

    Most cracked, rooted or separated pipes can be lined. Pipes that are fully collapsed or badly bellied may need bursting or traditional repair. A camera inspection tells us which method fits.

    Lining reduces the inside diameter slightly, but the smooth surface usually improves flow. Pipe bursting can maintain or increase the diameter.

    While trenchless methods are highly versatile, they are not suitable for all types of pipe damage. Severely collapsed pipes, pipes with large offsets, or pipes that have suffered extreme structural failure may still require traditional excavation. A professional camera inspection from FKM Plumbing will confirm what is possible.

    Trenchless pipe repairs, particularly CIPP lining, are designed to be extremely durable and long-lasting. The new pipe created inside the old one can have a lifespan of 50 years or more, often exceeding the expected life of the original piping. These repairs are resistant to corrosion, root intrusion, and chemical damage.

    CIPP (cured-in-place pipe) lining involves inserting a new liner inside the existing pipe to create a pipe within a pipe. Pipe bursting, on the other hand, involves breaking apart the old pipe while pulling a new pipe into its place, which leaves you with a completely new pipe.

    Yes, trenchless pipe repair is often considered more environmentally friendly than traditional methods. It significantly reduces excavation, preserving soil, vegetation, and natural habitats. It also minimizes soil disturbance and the amount of waste sent to landfills, as the old pipe material is often left in place.
