    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Riverside

    Small indoor grease traps typically cost $150 to $400 per cleaning. Large outdoor interceptors often run $300 to $1,200 depending on size and buildup. Regular maintenance plans can lower the cost per visit.

    FOG stands for Fats, Oils, and Grease. When poured down drains, FOG cools and solidifies, adhering to pipe walls and eventually leading to severe clogs. In grease traps, it separates from wastewater, but if not regularly removed, it overflows into the sewer system, causing blockages and environmental issues.

    The frequency of grease trap cleaning depends heavily on usage. Commercial kitchens often require cleaning every 1 to 3 months, while residential units might only need it once a year or less. Local Riverside health codes for restaurants might mandate specific intervals.

    The terms are often used interchangeably, but 'grease interceptor' typically refers to larger, outdoor units with capacities over 500 gallons, while 'grease trap' usually describes smaller, indoor units under 100 gallons, such as under-sink models. Both serve the same function of separating FOG from wastewater.

    Neglecting commercial grease trap cleaning in Riverside can lead to severe drain blockages, sewage backups, foul odors impacting your business, and potential fines from the local health department or wastewater authority. It can also cause costly plumbing emergencies and business downtime.

    Key signs include slow drains in your kitchen sink, gurgling noises from drains, foul odors coming from your drains or around the trap area, and visible FOG buildup when you inspect the trap. Ignoring these can lead to complete blockages.

    Indoor traps usually take 30 minutes to an hour. Outdoor interceptors can take one to two hours depending on size and how much buildup is present.
