    Residential Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Riverside

    For homeowners experiencing issues like a constantly running pump, short cycling, or uneven water pressure, a residential well pressure tank replacement is often the solution. Our team at FKM Plumbing quickly diagnoses the problem, whether it is a ruptured diaphragm, waterlogging, or corrosion, and replaces your old tank with a new, properly sized model. We ensure the new tank is installed correctly, with appropriate pre-charge pressure and system calibration, to restore reliable water service to your home.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Riverside, CA

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop, typically at 30/50 or 40/60 psi. Pitted contacts, insect nests and a clogged sensing nipple are common causes of failure in well houses. We replace worn switches with Square D or similar models and clean or replace the nipple below it. A low-pressure cutoff switch can also be added to stop the pump if the well runs dry. We set the switch to suit your new tank's pre-charge.

    Bladder Pressure Tank Replacement in Riverside

    Bladder tanks hold water inside a flexible butyl bag surrounded by pressurized air. When the bag ruptures, water fills the whole tank, there is no air cushion and the pump short cycles. A simple test is pressing the air valve on top, since water spitting out means the bladder has failed. We replace the tank, set the new pre-charge and verify the pump runs a full cycle. Most bladder tank swaps take about two to three hours.

    Tank Tee, Gauge and Relief Valve Replacement in Riverside

    The tank tee is the manifold that connects the tank, switch, gauge, relief valve and drain. Galvanized tees corrode and restrict flow, especially with sandy or mineral-heavy water. We replace them with brass or stainless steel tank tees that hold up much longer. A new liquid-filled gauge gives accurate readings, and a fresh relief valve protects against a stuck switch. These small parts make a big difference in how well the system holds up.

    Constant Pressure System Conversion in Riverside, CA

    A constant pressure system pairs a variable speed drive like the Franklin SubDrive or Grundfos SQE with a smaller pressure tank. The pump speeds up or slows down to hold a set pressure, so showers do not fade when a sprinkler kicks on. These systems cost more, often $2,500 to $5,000, but they end pressure swings. They also reduce hard starts on the motor. We explain whether your well and pump are good candidates.

    Well Pressure Tank Leak Repair vs. Replacement in Riverside

    When your well pressure tank shows signs of trouble, it is important to determine if a repair is feasible or if full replacement is necessary. Small air leaks or issues with the air valve might be repairable. However, if the tank itself is corroded, has a ruptured diaphragm, or is waterlogged, replacement is almost always the more cost-effective and reliable option. Our technicians in Riverside provide an honest assessment, explaining the pros and cons of repair versus a complete well pressure tank replacement, so you can make an informed decision.

    Common signs include your well pump cycling on and off very frequently (short-cycling), fluctuating water pressure at your faucets, no water coming from the tap, or a waterlogged tank when you tap on it. Visible rust or leaks around the tank are also clear indicators.

    It depends on your pump's flow rate and the drawdown needed for at least a one minute run time. We calculate this on site instead of guessing from the old tank.

    Most pressure tanks last 10 to 15 years. Hard water, sand and frequent cycling can shorten that, especially when the pre-charge is never checked.

    Both bladder and diaphragm tanks use an air cushion to pressurize water, but they differ in how the water and air are separated. Bladder tanks have a replaceable rubber bladder that holds the water, fully isolating it from the air. Diaphragm tanks use a fixed rubber diaphragm. Bladder tanks are often preferred for their longevity and ease of maintenance.

    Regular maintenance for a well pressure tank involves checking the air pressure in the tank at least once a year, ensuring it's properly pre-charged.
