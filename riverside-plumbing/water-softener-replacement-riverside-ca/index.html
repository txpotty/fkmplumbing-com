    Metered Softener Replacement in Riverside

    Older timer-based softeners regenerate on a set schedule whether you used water or not. Metered softeners track actual usage and regenerate only when the resin is nearly used up. This saves salt, water and wear on the system. We replace old timer valves with metered Fleck 5600SXT or Clack WS1 valves. Most homeowners notice lower salt use within the first few weeks.

    Resin Tank and Media Replacement in Riverside, CA

    Softener resin can break down after 10 to 15 years, especially with chlorinated city water. When resin fails, you will see hard water symptoms even though the system is regenerating. Sometimes replacing resin and the tank is more cost effective than a whole new system. We inspect the valve and brine tank to decide. If the valve is old, a full replacement often makes more sense.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Softener Replacement in Riverside

    The cost of water softener replacement in Riverside, CA can vary widely, typically ranging from $1,500 to $4,000. This depends on the type and size of the new system, labor costs and any needed plumbing changes. We provide clear, upfront pricing after assessing your needs.

    Most softener replacements take two to four hours. New loops or drain lines can take a bit longer.

    New water softeners usually come with two types of warranties. There is a manufacturer's warranty on the equipment itself, which can range from 5 to 10 years or more for tanks and control valves, and an installation warranty from FKM Plumbing. We stand behind our work with a solid warranty for your peace of mind.

    Salt-free conditioners reduce scale buildup but do not remove hardness minerals. A true softener removes them, which is better for soap and fixtures.

    Spots on dishes, scale on faucets, dry skin and increased soap use can signal a failing softener. A softener that no longer uses salt or keeps flooding the brine tank may also need replacement.

    A new, properly sized water softener can sometimes improve water pressure if your old unit was undersized or had heavy mineral buildup restricting flow. However, a water softener's main job is to remove hardness minerals, not to boost pressure. If low water pressure is your main concern, we can look into other causes.

    We test your hardness in grains per gallon and estimate daily water usage. That determines the resin capacity needed.

    To extend the life of your new water softener, use the correct type of salt, if applicable, and keep the brine tank filled. Regular maintenance, such as cleaning the brine tank and periodic inspections by a professional, can also help ensure its longevity and efficiency.
