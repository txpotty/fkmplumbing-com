    Understanding Hydrojetting vs. Snaking in Glendale, CA

    While snaking can punch a hole through a clog, hydrojetting in Glendale, CA provides a complete pipe cleaning. Snaking is effective for simple, localized clogs, but it usually leaves behind residual buildup that can lead to recurring problems. Hydrojetting, conversely, scours the entire interior of the pipe, removing debris, grease, and scale, offering a more lasting solution to your drainage issues.

    Main Sewer Line Jetting in Glendale, CA

    The main line from your home to the street carries everything, so buildup there causes backups in several fixtures. We jet from the nearest cleanout and work the nozzle all the way to the city tap. Afterward, a camera inspection shows whether the line is clean or has structural damage. Clean lines also give a clearer picture for any future sewer repairs.

    Kitchen Grease Line Jetting in Glendale

    Kitchen drains collect grease that cools and hardens along the pipe wall, especially in long horizontal runs under floors and slabs. Snaking clears a path, but the grease soon builds back. Jetting with a rotating nozzle peels that layer off and flushes it downstream. Homeowners who cook often and restaurants benefit most from regular kitchen line jetting.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Hydrojet Plumbing in Glendale

    The cost of hydrojet plumbing in Glendale, CA can range from $300 to $800 or more, depending on the severity and location of the clog, as well as the length and diameter of the pipe.     It is safe for most cast iron, clay, ABS and PVC lines in decent condition. We always inspect with a camera first and lower the pressure or recommend another method if the pipe is cracked or badly corroded. That step protects fragile lines.

    Key signs you might need hydrojet plumbing include often recurring clogs that snaking doesn't fully resolve, multiple slow drains throughout your home, gurgling sounds from drains or toilets, or foul odors coming from your drains. A severe sewer backup is also a clear indicator that hydrojetting is needed to clear the main line.

    Rental jetters exist, but high pressure can injure you or damage a weak pipe if used incorrectly. Without a camera you also cannot tell whether the line is clean or broken. Professional jetting is safer and generally more effective.

    Most residential jobs take one to three hours, including camera inspections. Commercial lines with multiple runs can take a half day or more. We give you an estimate when we book.

    Most homes only need jetting every few years or when clogs recur. Restaurants and busy commercial kitchens often benefit from jetting every three to six months. We recommend a schedule based on what the camera shows.

    We use professional cart and trailer jetters from manufacturers like Spartan and RIDGID, paired with rotating, penetrating and root-cutting nozzles. Our cameras record video of the line before and after. This equipment lets us handle both homes and commercial buildings.

    When performed by a trained professional, hydrojetting is generally safe for most types of pipes, including older cast iron or PVC.
